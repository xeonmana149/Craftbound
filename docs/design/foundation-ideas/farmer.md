# Farmer — Plant now, decide what grows

**Sow** costs 2 XP and plants a 0/2 token named Crop (the Wheat token). At the start of your next turn, surviving Wheat destroys itself and gives you +1 maximum XP, up to 10. Your opponent can destroy it first. A Farmer needs a reason to plant at 10 XP too. Grazers can feed while Wheat is on the board, and several cards reward field size before the Crops mature.

One deck spends early turns on growth and wins with a few expensive mobs. Another grows fast-maturing Crops for cards and uses Animals to contest the board. **Ready** uses current Workshop effects. **Pair** also needs a named candidate from this chapter. **New** needs the logic indicated. Mob stats are Attack/Health and tool stats are Attack/Durability.

## How the decks play

**Ramp with a window to respond.** Sow on turn 2 with an open block slot. If Wheat survives, turn 3 normally starts with 4 maximum XP. That buys a Dozing Sow a turn early. Under pressure, play Farm Dog on turn 2 instead. The dog buys time and makes the opponent engage the Shield mob before attacking a Crop.

**Build a pasture.** Play a Turnip Patch beside a damaged Pasture Ewe. The Ewe heals at the end of your turn while the Crop is still there. If the patch survives, you draw when it matures next turn. Bean Trellis is the alternative when you want a Crop to stay on the board. Neutral Cow and other Animals gain from Village Shepherd. The Tameable Farm Dog and Scarecrow Golem use different families and miss those buffs.

**Cash in, or wait.** With two Crops and 6 XP you can play Flock Bell for two Ewes. With one endangered Crop and only 4 XP, Fallow Field costs 2 and returns 3 spendable XP. That gets you to 5 immediately while giving up whatever that Crop would have produced next turn. It never increases your maximum XP. A Farmer at 10 can still use the same trade to squeeze in another play after spending XP.

**The expensive draws should do different jobs.** Harvest Caravan refills a hand when Animals have survived. Hayrick Guardian can defend when you’re behind. The Great Aurochs presents a Shield and a second body when there is room, and gives surviving Animals a way to close the game. Starting a deck with two of everything above 6 XP is a good way to lose before the first Wheat matures.

**Counterplay and first tests.** Ranged mobs threaten Wheat behind an ordinary front row. A direct block removal effect bypasses that row too. Playtest each expensive closer in a hand with no ramp, and track how often Deep Furrows yields both XP increases. A full back row wastes new plantings. If Deep Furrows routinely makes turns 5–7 trivial, drop its card draw first.

**Workshop timing.** For Turnip Patch, put “draw one” and then “destroy self” under the same start-of-turn timing. A Crop destroyed early by damage or Fallow Field misses that payout. “Matures” in the proposed Seed Vault text would explicitly mean a Crop completing its own start-of-turn growth effect. To count it reliably the engine needs a maturation event; the current Sow power doesn’t fire the old Harvest trigger. Start the first playtest with Wheat, Turnip Patch, Farm Dog, Pasture Ewe and Prize Bull.
