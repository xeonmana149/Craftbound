# Redstoner — Foundation concepts

**Hero power — Power Up, 2 XP.** Choose one of your blocks with **Activate** and resolve its Activate text. The hero power is usable once per turn. Playing an Activate block does not itself use the power or automatically fire the ability. A full back row has only three slots, so even a cheap new machine competes with whatever is already installed.

Redstoner wins by choosing which machine to power: the Dropper for cards, the Tripwire for bodies, or the Piston to save a mob. A second machine gives options without doubling output. Mobs keep the front row occupied and provide a fallback hand when no machine appears. The class is weakest when it buys a block and cannot afford the 2 XP to fire it before an opposing Ranged attacker reaches the back row.

**Curve.** 1 XP × 6 · 2 XP × 10 · 3 XP × 10 · 4 XP × 6 · 5 XP × 3 · 6 XP × 1 · 7 XP × 2 · 8 XP × 1 · 9 XP × 1.

**Rarity mix.** 18 Common, 14 Rare, 6 Epic, 2 Legendary. Stats are Attack/Health for mobs, Health for blocks, and Attack/Durability for tools. **Ready** uses current Workshop rules. **Pair** also needs another named proposal in this chapter. **New** needs the implementation described after the table.

**How it plays.** On turn two you can place Tripwire Hook. On turn three, Power Up it to deal 1 to every enemy mob, then use Stone Button to finish a damaged mob for 1 more while you still have a block. If the opposing board is empty on turn five, play Dropper and power it immediately to draw instead. Either option leaves the other machine installed for next turn.

**Two builds to try.** A Golem deck wants bodies on the board before it plays Powered Rail or Daylight Clock. The Walking Foundry gives that list a late threat even when the block row is full and its Dropper cannot enter. A control deck uses Sticky Piston to rebuy Rail Cart Courier’s draw, keeping the rest of its mob row empty to make the random return predictable. An opponent can attack the Courier first or use Ranged to hit the Piston directly. Emergency Shutdown takes down your own machines as well, giving a real cost to the reset.

**Implementation boundaries.** The existing Workshop supports **Activate** as a block trigger. The proposed **Relay** action means “resolve one friendly block’s Activate text.” Lever Pull needs a chosen-block action that invokes that trigger. Latch Key needs a random eligible-block version. Count activations on the affected block rather than global hero-power presses. Pulse Counter needs a persistent per-block count, and Tuned Observer needs a distinct “another block was activated by your hero power” event. Settle a Relay before starting a new one and forbid a block from re-entering the same activation chain. Cirra needs a count of successful returns; a full hand or destroyed token must not count as returned. Summoning a Workshop Golem does not fire its Spawn effect.

**First balance check.** Empty-hand draws can make Dropper the default button press forever. Count how often a player with two active machines actually chooses the other one. Cheap card draw should lose to pressure on the 4-Health Dropper. Try Lever Pull at 1 XP only after a full pass with the built-in hero power. If spare activations dominate, raise Lever Pull to 2 XP before weakening every individual block.
