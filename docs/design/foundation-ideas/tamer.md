# Tamer — Make room for the pack

**Call Pup** costs 2 XP and summons a 1/1 Wolf Pup. A Pup can trade for a damaged attacker, but calling one into an empty board every turn loses ground. These cards offer reasons to spend that 2 XP: a wary opponent must decide whether to spend a removal card on the trainer, the unusually sturdy pet, or several small attackers.

Wolf Pup is an existing **Tameable** token. The current card system gives each mob one family. A Tameable and an Animal are separate targets, so these effects say exactly which family they help. **Ready** means current rules can express the idea. **Pair** needs another collectible idea from this chapter. **New** needs an extension to the engine or editor. A mob’s stats are Attack/Health, a tool’s are Attack/Durability, and Blocks list Health.

### Two decks to try

**Small pack.** Open with Scruffy Stray, then threaten Pack Listener once you have two bodies. A saved 0-XP Wolf Pup from Stray’s Drop lets you play a buff and refill on the same turn. Spend turn four on Pack Rally only when its buffs change a trade; recovering with Rally after an empty board gives you just one 2/2 Pup. An opponent can clear the pack first or remove the Listener and shrink every attacker.

**Caravan.** Protect Trail Handler with Rain-Shelter Wolf, then use the resulting Pup as a cheap recipient for Grey Collar. At 6 XP, Mountain Caravan gives two 2/5 bodies. It does **not** draw cards: a **Spawn** triggers when the card is played from hand. Whistle Recall lets you spend another 4 XP playing one Llama for its draw. Aim Recall at a collectible companion: returning a token such as a Wolf Pup removes it from play without putting it in your hand. This line has staying power but lets the opponent dictate which mob trades first.

**First playtest.** Track how often Call Pup, Forest Kennel, and Mossback leave an eighth mob waiting for space. With six mobs in play, Mossback takes the last slot herself; both pups fail to enter. For capture prototypes, a captured mob should enter exhausted and should not fire **Spawn**. Quiet Hand targets Animals only. Kestrel’s two-Pup fallback makes it usable against decks with no Tameables.
