Craftbound   /   Foundation design notebook

Foundation Field Notes

360 candidates for nine classes. Each chapter starts with a decision you want the player to face. The stats are starting points for testing; none of these ideas has been added to the game.

How to use this pool. Pick one early play, one payoff, and one way an opponent can interrupt them. Test that interaction with familiar Neutral cards before adding a second package. “Existing Workshop rules” can still require another proposed card, token or pool to be authored first. A proposed keyword is a design shorthand until the engine supports its exact timing.
Nine mechanics to test before printing a new keyword

Use a name only if it shortens several cards. These are design suggestions, not nine new rules already in the mod.

  • Fighter · Breakthrough. Shorthand for the existing “after this attacks, destroys a mob or block, and survives” trigger. A defensive kill does not count.
  • Builder · Blueprint. Pick one of three different blocks from your deck and draw it. Start on Blueprint Chest; face-up choices matter when there are only three block slots.
  • Farmer · Mature. A Crop completes its own growth effect. Destroying it early cuts the harvest short. A new shared event would let Seed Vault recognise a successful harvest.
  • Fisherman · Haul. The first time each turn you add a card to your hand without drawing it. Catching a fish directly onto the board is not a Haul.
  • Alchemist · Dose. After your second Action resolves this turn. A hero power or a third potion doesn’t trigger it again.
  • Miner · Vein. An extra payoff only when you deliberately mine this Ore. Its normal Drop still happens when an opponent destroys it.
  • Chef · Well Fed. Already supported: healing beyond full Health raises maximum Health. Test it alongside a payoff for ordinary healing so healing a wounded mob stays worthwhile.
  • Tamer · Tame. Take control of the specified enemy mob if there’s a free slot. Test exhausted arrivals that do not repeat Spawn, and give rare narrow capture effects a fallback.
  • Redstoner · Relay. Resolve another friendly block’s Activate text. Prevent a machine from re-entering the same chain so two relays cannot activate one another forever.

Fighter

Hero power & class identity

Craft costs 2 XP and equips a 1 Attack / 1 Durability Wooden Sword. It replaces your current tool. That makes a one-hit weapon worth building around: spend its last swing, then decide whether to craft again or equip something better. The other starting point is an Illager that lives long enough to receive the next buff. Both plans win with combat on the board. Fighter gets very little healing and no board-wide damage in this pool.

The current collection already supplies Stone Sword, Anvil, Looting, Ominous Horn, Raid Drummer, Pillager Scout, Illager Swordsman and Raiding Party. These are forty additional candidates, with new names and a different job where an existing effect is close. Ready means current effect primitives can express the design. Pair needs the named card in this idea pool. New identifies missing logic before you try to build the card. Mob stats are Attack/Health, tool stats are Attack/Durability, and Blocks list Health.

Playing the deck

Two decks to try

Patrol formation. Run a dense Illager curve alongside your existing Raid Drummer. Putting Flag Detail on a fresh Outpost Recruit turns the 1/3 into a 4/2. Putting it on a mob at 1 Health kills that mob. Axe-Break Veteran can gain another point of Attack from surviving the hit, and Outpost Flagbearer helps fragile Illagers survive it. Save Hilda for a turn when partners entered play and need Sprint; an Illager already on the board since last turn usually needs the Attack bonus more. The Last Reserve counts before summoning and fills at most seven mob slots.

One more swing. Use Craft to remove a 1-Health mob without spending a card. On turn three, Craft plus Axe Whetstone makes a single 2-damage swing. A bigger route is turn two Timber Hatchet, one swing, then turn three Quickdraw Bracer and a second swing while an Illager is in play. A Tool triggers its attached effects on the final swing. An enemy Fox threatens whatever Durability you save.

First playtest. Compare two games with the same opening hand. In one, Craft immediately; in the other, play an Illager and spend turn two improving it. Track whether the Fighter wins through a surviving board or an uninterrupted string of hero attacks. If Tools finish too many games without mob combat, cut Durability on the repeatable Tool before weakening the underlying hero power. Both Legendaries give an opponent something to answer: Hilda needs teammates, and the Axe needs targets it can kill.

Playing the deck

How the decks play

Golem formation. Play Copper Sentinel on turn 3. On turn 4, trade into a 2-Attack mob and use Build afterward. If the Sentinel is your only mob, it gets the Wall’s Health buff. Buttress Golem lets it trade up on the next turn. Neutral Iron Golem is another body for those buffs. Removing the Buttress immediately removes its Attack aura.

Workshop economy. Falsework needs two open block slots to produce both pieces. If it survives to your next turn, it replaces itself with a draw and leaves the Wall behind. Later, use Site Surveyor before Hoist Operator. With three blocks you draw three, then pick which collectible block to take back. Returning a token Wall removes it from play instead. A full back row makes another Build impossible until you clear space.

Closing the game. Ironwork Assembly refills an empty mob row. The Walking Keep rewards Golems that survived the previous turn. It only buffs other Golems already in play, so it’s much weaker after a board clear. Hesta offers a third approach if implemented. Bank Health on one block, then cash it in for one large attacker. Like any new mob without Sprint, that Golem gives the opponent a turn to respond.

Counterplay and first tests. Pressure early, send Ranged attacks into the back row when there is no defending Shield mob, or save direct block removal for Bell Foundry. Check how often Wall-only opening hands can contest turn 3, whether an uncontested Foundry fills both the board and hand too easily, and how many cards Site Surveyor actually draws. If the Foundry snowballs, test a start-of-turn summon so the opponent can answer the first Courier.

First build. Implement Copper Sentinel, Cobble Courier, Site Surveyor, Hoist Operator and Ironwork Assembly together. That small group tests front-row protection, the value of a third structure, a way to make space and a payoff for keeping the workshop running.

Hero power & class identity

Playing the deck

How the decks play

Ramp with a window to respond. Sow on turn 2 with an open block slot. If Wheat survives, turn 3 normally starts with 4 maximum XP. That buys a Dozing Sow a turn early. Under pressure, play Farm Dog on turn 2 instead. The dog buys time and makes the opponent engage the Shield mob before attacking a Crop.

Build a pasture. Play a Turnip Patch beside a damaged Pasture Ewe. The Ewe heals at the end of your turn while the Crop is still there. If the patch survives, you draw when it matures next turn. Bean Trellis is the alternative when you want a Crop to stay on the board. Neutral Cow and other Animals gain from Village Shepherd. The Tameable Farm Dog and Scarecrow Golem use different families and miss those buffs.

Cash in, or wait. With two Crops and 6 XP you can play Flock Bell for two Ewes. With one endangered Crop and only 4 XP, Fallow Field costs 2 and returns 3 spendable XP. That gets you to 5 immediately while giving up whatever that Crop would have produced next turn. It never increases your maximum XP. A Farmer at 10 can still use the same trade to squeeze in another play after spending XP.

The expensive draws should do different jobs. Harvest Caravan refills a hand when Animals have survived. Hayrick Guardian can defend when you’re behind. The Great Aurochs presents a Shield and a second body when there is room, and gives surviving Animals a way to close the game. Starting a deck with two of everything above 6 XP is a good way to lose before the first Wheat matures.

Counterplay and first tests. Ranged mobs threaten Wheat behind an ordinary front row. A direct block removal effect bypasses that row too. Playtest each expensive closer in a hand with no ramp, and track how often Deep Furrows yields both XP increases. A full back row wastes new plantings. If Deep Furrows routinely makes turns 5–7 trivial, drop its card draw first.

Workshop timing. For Turnip Patch, put “draw one” and then “destroy self” under the same start-of-turn timing. A Crop destroyed early by damage or Fallow Field misses that payout. “Matures” in the proposed Seed Vault text would explicitly mean a Crop completing its own start-of-turn growth effect. To count it reliably the engine needs a maturation event; the current Sow power doesn’t fire the old Harvest trigger. Start the first playtest with Wheat, Turnip Patch, Farm Dog, Pasture Ewe and Prize Bull.

Fisherman

Jump to deck notes ↓
Hero power & class identity

Hero power — What a Catch!, 2 XP. Summon a random Cod (1/3), Salmon (2/1) or Pufferfish (1/1). All three are Aquatic. When Pufferfish dies to an enemy mob, that killer loses 2 Attack through the end of its controller’s next turn. Catching a fish places it on the board; it does not trigger Schooling, which specifically watches Aquatic mobs played from hand.

The Fisherman deck has two jobs: put bodies in the water, then decide which fish to keep and which to trade. A small catch makes a good buff target or buys a turn for a fragile Schooling attacker. Rods and return-to-hand effects make a second, slower “fill the creel” build possible. Its reach against blocks comes from Ranged mobs; it has little direct removal and few answers to wide boards.

Curve. 1 XP × 5 · 2 XP × 10 · 3 XP × 9 · 4 XP × 7 · 5 XP × 3 · 6 XP × 2 · 7 XP × 2 · 8 XP × 1 · 9 XP × 1.

Rarity mix. 18 Common, 14 Rare, 6 Epic, 2 Legendary. Stats are Attack/Health for mobs, Health for blocks, and Attack/Durability for tools. Ready uses current Workshop rules. Pair also needs another named proposal in this chapter. New needs the implementation described after the table. The fish tokens from What a Catch! are already available.

Playing the deck

Two decks to try

Small pack. Open with Scruffy Stray, then threaten Pack Listener once you have two bodies. A saved 0-XP Wolf Pup from Stray’s Drop lets you play a buff and refill on the same turn. Spend turn four on Pack Rally only when its buffs change a trade; recovering with Rally after an empty board gives you just one 2/2 Pup. Caravan. Protect Trail Handler with Rain-Shelter Wolf, then use the resulting Pup as a cheap recipient for Grey Collar. At 6 XP, Mountain Caravan gives two 2/5 bodies. It does not draw cards: a Spawn triggers when the card is played from hand. Whistle Recall lets you spend another 4 XP playing one Llama for its draw. Aim Recall at a collectible companion: returning a token such as a Wolf Pup removes it from play without putting it in your hand. This line has staying power but lets the opponent dictate which mob trades first.

Hero power & class identity

Hero power — Power Up, 2 XP. Choose one of your blocks with Activate and resolve its Activate text. The hero power is usable once per turn. Playing an Activate block does not itself use the power or automatically fire the ability. A full back row has only three slots, so even a cheap new machine competes with whatever is already installed.

Redstoner wins by choosing which machine to power: the Dropper for cards, the Tripwire for bodies, or the Piston to save a mob. A second machine gives options without doubling output. Mobs keep the front row occupied and provide a fallback hand when no machine appears. The class is weakest when it buys a block and cannot afford the 2 XP to fire it before an opposing Ranged attacker reaches the back row.

Curve. 1 XP × 6 · 2 XP × 10 · 3 XP × 10 · 4 XP × 6 · 5 XP × 3 · 6 XP × 1 · 7 XP × 2 · 8 XP × 1 · 9 XP × 1.

Rarity mix. 18 Common, 14 Rare, 6 Epic, 2 Legendary. Stats are Attack/Health for mobs, Health for blocks, and Attack/Durability for tools. Ready uses current Workshop rules. Pair also needs another named proposal in this chapter. New needs the implementation described after the table.

Playing the deck

How it plays. On turn two you can place Tripwire Hook. On turn three, Power Up it to deal 1 to every enemy mob, then use Stone Button to finish a damaged mob for 1 more while you still have a block. If the opposing board is empty on turn five, play Dropper and power it immediately to draw instead. Either option leaves the other machine installed for next turn.

Two builds to try. A Golem deck wants bodies on the board before it plays Powered Rail or Daylight Clock. The Walking Foundry gives that list a late threat even when the block row is full and its Dropper cannot enter. A control deck uses Sticky Piston to rebuy Rail Cart Courier’s draw, keeping the rest of its mob row empty to make the random return predictable. An opponent can attack the Courier first or use Ranged to hit the Piston directly. Emergency Shutdown takes down your own machines as well, giving a real cost to the reset.

Implementation boundaries. The existing Workshop supports Activate as a block trigger. The proposed Relay action means “resolve one friendly block’s Activate text.” Lever Pull needs a chosen-block action that invokes that trigger. Latch Key needs a random eligible-block version. Count activations on the affected block rather than global hero-power presses. Pulse Counter needs a persistent per-block count, and Tuned Observer needs a distinct “another block was activated by your hero power” event. Settle a Relay before starting a new one and forbid a block from re-entering the same activation chain. Cirra needs a count of successful returns; a full hand or destroyed token must not count as returned. Summoning a Workshop Golem does not fire its Spawn effect.

First balance check. Empty-hand draws can make Dropper the default button press forever. Count how often a player with two active machines actually chooses the other one. Cheap card draw should lose to pressure on the 4-Health Dropper. Try Lever Pull at 1 XP only after a full pass with the built-in hero power. If spare activations dominate, raise Lever Pull to 2 XP before weakening every individual block.
